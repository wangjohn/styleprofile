# Setting Up a Simple Home Backup Routine

Hard drives fail, laptops get stolen, and files get deleted by accident. A basic backup routine takes under an hour to set up and then mostly runs itself.

## The 3-2-1 rule

A widely used guideline for backups:

- **3** copies of your data (the original plus two backups)
- **2** different types of storage (for example, an external drive and cloud storage)
- **1** copy stored off-site, away from your home

You do not need anything fancy to meet this rule.

## Step 1: Decide what to back up

Start with files that cannot be replaced:

- Documents, tax records, and scans
- Photos and videos
- Email archives and password manager exports
- Creative or work projects

Operating systems and apps can be reinstalled, so they matter less unless you want a full system image.

## Step 2: Choose your backup destinations

**Local backup**
- An external hard drive or SSD works well. Pick one with at least twice the capacity of the data you are saving.
- Built-in tools make this easy: Time Machine on Mac, File History or Windows Backup on Windows.

**Off-site or cloud backup**
- Services such as Backblaze, iDrive, or a cloud storage plan from your existing provider copy files over the internet.
- Look for versioning, which lets you recover earlier copies of a file.
- Consider encryption, especially for sensitive documents.

## Step 3: Automate it

Manual backups get forgotten. Schedule them instead:

1. Turn on your operating system's backup tool and select the external drive.
2. Install the cloud backup client and choose the folders to upload.
3. Set both to run daily or whenever the drive is connected.

## Step 4: Keep the external drive somewhere safe

If the drive sits next to your computer, a fire, flood, or burglary can destroy both. Store it in a different room, or swap between two drives and keep one at a relative's home or at work.

## Step 5: Test your backups

A backup you have never restored is only a guess. Once a quarter:

- Open the backup and confirm recent files are there.
- Restore one or two files to a temporary folder to check that they open correctly.
- Look for error messages or warnings in the backup software.

## A sample weekly routine

- **Daily:** automatic cloud backup and local backup
- **Monthly:** check that the backup completed without errors
- **Quarterly:** restore a test file
- **Yearly:** review what you are backing up and replace drives older than four or five years

## Common mistakes

- Relying only on a sync service such as a shared folder. Syncing mirrors deletions and corruption, so it is not a true backup.
- Using one drive as the only copy.
- Forgetting phones and tablets, which often have their own backup settings.

Once these pieces are in place, the routine becomes background maintenance, and your files stay recoverable.
