## Reading a Nutrition Label: A Quick Guide

Knowing where to look makes it easy to compare foods.

### Start with serving size

Everything on the label is based on one serving, not the whole package. If a bag lists three servings and you eat it all, multiply every number by three. 
### Check the calories

Calories measure the energy in one serving. Roughly, 40 is low, 100 is moderate, and 400 is high.

### Look at the nutrients

- **Limit these:** saturated fat, sodium, and added sugars. Most people eat more of them than recommended.
- **Get enough of these:** dietary fiber, vitamin D, calcium, iron, and potassium.
- **Total fat and carbohydrates** are worth noting, but the subtypes underneath tell you more. Trans fat should be as close to zero as possible.

### Use the % Daily Value

The % Daily Value shows how much a serving contributes to a typical 2,000-calorie daily diet.

- **5% or less** is low
- **20% or more** is high

Aim low for saturated fat, sodium, and added sugars, and high for fiber and vitamins.

### Read the ingredient list too

Ingredients are listed from most to least by weight. If sugar appears in the first few spots, or under names like corn syrup, dextrose, or maltose, the product is likely sweeter than it looks.

### A simple routine

1. Check serving size.
2. Scan calories.
3. Compare the % Daily Value for nutrients to limit and to get more of.
4. Glance at the ingredients.
