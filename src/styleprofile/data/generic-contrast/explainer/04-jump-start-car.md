# How to Jump-Start a Car

A dead battery is common and usually fixable in a few minutes with jumper cables and a second vehicle. Following the correct order matters, because it keeps sparks away from the battery and protects both cars' electronics.

## What you need

- A set of jumper cables (red for positive, black for negative)
- A running vehicle with a battery of the same voltage, usually 12 volts
- Gloves and eye protection, if available
- Your owner's manual, in case your car has special jump points

## Before you connect anything

1. Park the working car close enough for the cables to reach, but do not let the vehicles touch.
2. Turn off both engines and switch off lights, radio, and other accessories.
3. Set both cars to park (or neutral for a manual) and engage the parking brakes.
4. Inspect the dead battery. If it is cracked, leaking, or frozen, do not attempt a jump.
5. Locate the terminals. The positive terminal is marked with a plus sign and often has a red cover.

## Connecting the cables

Follow this order exactly:

1. Attach one **red clamp** to the **positive terminal** of the dead battery.
2. Attach the other red clamp to the **positive terminal** of the good battery.
3. Attach one **black clamp** to the **negative terminal** of the good battery.
4. Attach the last black clamp to **unpainted bare metal** on the dead car, such as an engine bracket or a bolt, away from the battery. This is your ground.

Using bare metal for the final connection helps prevent sparks near battery gases.

## Starting the engines

1. Start the working car and let it run for two to five minutes.
2. Try to start the dead car. If it does not turn over, wait a few more minutes and try again.
3. Once it starts, leave it running.

## Removing the cables

Reverse the order so the final connection is the first to come off:

1. Remove the black clamp from the grounded metal.
2. Remove the black clamp from the good battery.
3. Remove the red clamp from the good battery.
4. Remove the red clamp from the formerly dead battery.

Do not let the clamps touch each other while any of them are attached.

## After the jump

- Drive for at least 20 to 30 minutes so the alternator can recharge the battery.
- Avoid shutting off the engine right away.
- If the car dies again, the battery may be worn out, or the alternator may be failing. Have it tested, since many auto parts stores do this for free.

## When not to jump-start

Skip it if the battery is damaged, if you smell rotten eggs, or if your owner's manual warns against it. Many hybrids and electric vehicles have specific procedures, so check the manual first. Roadside assistance is a reasonable option if you feel unsure.
