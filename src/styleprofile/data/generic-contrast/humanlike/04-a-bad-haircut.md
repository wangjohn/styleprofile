Let me tell you about the worst forty minutes I've ever spent in a chair.

I was twenty-six and had just started a new job, and I decided, with the unearned confidence of someone who has watched too many makeover shows, that I needed a fresh look. My usual barber was on vacation. A place had opened near the office with a neon sign and a name I won't repeat, and the appointment was cheap, so I figured, how bad could it be?

Very bad. That's how bad.

The stylist was young and enthusiastic and did not look at me once. She looked at her own reflection, mostly, and at her phone, which was propped against the mirror playing a video about someone's cat. I said I wanted it a little shorter on the sides, same on top. She said "totally" in a tone that I should have recognized as the sound of a person not listening.

Then came the clippers. A lot of clippers. I watched piles of my hair fall in the cape and thought, that seems like more than a little. Around minute twenty-five I started to sweat. By minute thirty-five she stepped back, tilted her head like a painter, and said, "Okay, so, it's kind of bold."

Bold. Sure. It was a bowl, basically. A bowl with a notch.

I paid, and I even tipped, because I'm a coward who was raised to be polite. Then I walked out into the sunshine and caught my reflection in a shop window and made a small noise I hadn't known I could make.

The next morning was the real test, because I had to walk into the office. I wore a hat until ten, when my manager asked me, very kindly, to take it off for a team photo. The photo exists. It is on a wall somewhere. A coworker named Priya looked at me for a long second and said, "Brave choice," and honestly that was the nicest thing anyone said all week.

Hair grows, which is the one mercy in all this. About a half inch a month. I counted. It took roughly eight months for me to stop thinking about it every time I passed a mirror, and I went back to my regular barber the moment he returned, hat in hand, and told him everything. He laughed so hard he had to sit down.

Here's what I took from it. Never trust a stylist who won't make eye contact, and never say "a little shorter" without holding up two fingers to show exactly how little. Also, cats on a phone screen are not a good sign. I still think about that cat, though. It looked like it was having a better day than I was.
