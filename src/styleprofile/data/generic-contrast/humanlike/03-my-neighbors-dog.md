The dog next door is named Biscuit, and Biscuit hates me.

Not in a scary way. He's a medium-sized, wiry sort of mutt with one ear that stands up and one that doesn't, and he stares at me through the fence slats every morning like I owe him money. If I'm taking out the trash, he stares. If I'm just standing on my porch with coffee, minding my own business, he stares harder.

I tried being friendly. I said good morning. I tried treats, which he accepted, ate, and then went right back to staring, which felt like a pretty cold transaction. His owner, a retired mail carrier named Dolores, just laughs and says he's judging my character.

Here's the twist, though. Last winter I slipped on the ice by the driveway and lay there for a minute, more embarrassed than hurt. Biscuit went off like a fire alarm. He barked until Dolores came running, and she got me up and inside and made me tea.

Now when he stares, I think I get it. He isn't suspicious of me. He's keeping watch, and I'm just on his list.

I still say good morning every day. Yesterday he wagged once. Just once, but I counted it.
