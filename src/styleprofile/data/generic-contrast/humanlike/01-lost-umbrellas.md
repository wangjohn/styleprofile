I have lost, by my best estimate, eleven umbrellas. Maybe twelve.

It never happens in the rain, which is the strange part. I carry the thing like a loyal soldier through the whole downpour, shake it out under the awning of the coffee shop, and then walk away while it leans against the wall looking hopeful. By the time the sky clears I'm three blocks off, feeling light and clever, and I don't notice the missing weight in my hand until the next cloud rolls in.

The cheap ones go first. Those gas station umbrellas with the flimsy ribs that flip inside out the second a breeze shows up. I don't even grieve those. But I once bought a nice one, wooden handle, proper spring, the kind a person with their life together would own. I lost it in nine days. Nine.

My sister says the umbrellas aren't lost, they're just reassigned. Somewhere there's a guy on a train who has quietly built a collection and believes he's the luckiest man alive.

I've started tying a bright orange ribbon to the handle. It looks ridiculous. It also hasn't left my sight in three weeks, which might be a record, so I'm keeping the ribbon and my dignity can wait outside.
