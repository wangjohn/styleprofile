# Why I Switched to a Paper Planner

For about eight years I ran my life out of a calendar app. Every meeting, deadline, and dentist appointment lived on my phone, and it all synced neatly across my devices. By any practical measure it worked. So I was a little surprised when, last January, I bought a plain spiral-bound planner and started writing things down by hand.

The trigger was small. I opened my phone to check a time and, twenty minutes later, realized I had answered two emails, skimmed a news story, and never looked at the time at all. The calendar was inside the same device as every distraction I was trying to avoid, and it kept pulling me out of my day.

The planner has no notifications. It cannot ping me or tempt me. When I open it, I see the week laid out on two facing pages, and nothing else competes for my attention. I have found that I plan more thoughtfully, because writing takes effort and I do not waste it on tasks that do not matter. Crossing out a finished item is also strangely satisfying in a way that tapping a checkbox never was.

It is not perfect. I cannot share it with my partner, and I have missed a few appointments by leaving it at home. I keep a simple shared calendar for those. But for my own days, the paper version has made me calmer and more deliberate, and I do not expect to go back.
