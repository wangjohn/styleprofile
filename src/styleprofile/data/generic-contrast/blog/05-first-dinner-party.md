# Lessons from Hosting My First Dinner Party

Last month I invited six people to my apartment for dinner. I had cooked for friends before, but never for a proper sit-down evening with courses, place settings, and the quiet pressure of wanting everyone to have a good time. I spent a week planning and an afternoon panicking. Here is what I learned.

The first lesson is that the menu should be boring to you and impressive to nobody. I initially planned a complicated braise, a soufflé, and handmade pasta. Thankfully a friend talked me out of it. I made a roast chicken with potatoes, a big salad, and a simple fruit tart I had baked twice before. Nothing went wrong, because nothing was new. A dinner party is not the moment to test a recipe.

Second, do as much as possible before anyone arrives. I made the tart the night before, chopped vegetables in the morning, and set the table by mid-afternoon. When the doorbell rang, I was calm enough to actually greet people instead of shouting from the kitchen. Guests relax when the host is relaxed, and the reverse is just as true.

Third, the food matters less than I feared. I spent days worrying about whether the chicken would be dry. It was slightly overdone, and not one person mentioned it. What they talked about, for nearly three hours, was a story about someone's disastrous camping trip, and whether pineapple belongs on pizza. People remember being comfortable and amused far longer than they remember seasoning.

Fourth, let people help. I used to believe that accepting help meant admitting failure. When a friend offered to pour drinks, I said yes, and she turned out to be better at it than I would have been. Another guest cleared plates without being asked. Small tasks give guests something to do with their hands, and they make the evening feel shared rather than performed.

Finally, expect a mess and make peace with it. By the end, my kitchen looked as if a minor storm had passed through. Wine had been spilled, a candle had dripped on the tablecloth, and the dishes took an hour the next morning. None of it mattered. The mess was just proof that people had been there, eating and laughing.

I am already planning the next one, with a slightly more ambitious tart.
