## What you will actually be doing

As field coordinator, you are the link between our clients, our crews, and our surveyors. Concretely, your responsibilities will include:

1. Scheduling site visits and confirming access with property owners.
2. Preparing field packets before each job: maps, permits, prior survey data, and equipment checklists.
3. Making sure crews return with complete and well-labeled data.
4. Updating clients on progress and answering their routine questions.
5. Tracking equipment, from total stations to GPS rovers, and arranging maintenance.

You do not need to know all of this on day one. For your first two weeks, you will shadow Dawn, who held the role previously and now leads our residential projects. She will walk you through a typical job from first inquiry to final delivery. By week three, we expect you to handle one small project with Dawn nearby. By the end of your second month, you should be running a few jobs on your own. These are guidelines, not tests.

## How we work

A few things about the way this team operates may save you some confusion.

**We write things down.** Verbal agreements with clients get a follow-up email the same day. It protects everyone.

**We ask early.** Nobody here will think less of you for asking a question twice, or for saying you are not sure. A small error caught on Tuesday is far better than a large one discovered after the crew has driven two hours.

**Safety comes first.** If you ever feel a site is unsafe, you can stop work and call Hector, no explanation required. We would much rather lose a morning than risk an injury.

**Hours are flexible within reason.** Core hours are 9 to 3 on office days. Field days start earlier, often at 7, and we try to give you at least two days' notice. Overtime is paid, and we do not expect anyone to answer messages on evenings or weekends unless there is a true emergency.

## Practical details

**Pay and benefits.** You are paid on the 15th and the last day of each month. Health insurance begins on the first of the month after your start date, and Priyanka will walk you through the options. We offer fifteen days of paid time off in your first year, plus ten company holidays. You may begin using time off after your first ninety days, though if you have something already planned, tell me now and we will sort it out.

**Lunch.** There is a small kitchen with a fridge, microwave, and a very good espresso machine that Tom treats as a family member. On Fridays, we usually order in.

**Parking and commuting.** If you cycle, there is a secure rack in the back. We also offer a transit subsidy of $60 per month.

**Professional development.** We set aside $1,000 per person each year for training, conferences, or courses. Dawn is studying for a drone pilot license on this budget, and you might want to think about what you would like to learn.
