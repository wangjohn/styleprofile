# The Gate Latch: Willow Lane Community Garden, October

Frost is coming. The forecast says Thursday night might bring the first light one, so before you read anything else, go pick your tomatoes. Green ones are fine; they will ripen on a windowsill in a paper bag with a banana. The basil, sadly, has already given up, and if yours is blackened, pull it and add it to the compost.

## What happened in September

It was a strong month. The squash harvest was the best we have had in years, and the shared table by the gate was so loaded with zucchini that Gideon started leaving them in people's cars as a joke. (He denies this. We have photos.) Our donation bin sent 212 pounds of produce to the Eastside Pantry, which beats our previous record by almost forty pounds.

The September work party drew twenty-three people, who cleared the back fence line of bindweed, laid new cedar chips on the main path, and finally replaced the rotted gate post that has been leaning since last winter. Thank you to everyone who hauled wheelbarrows. Special thanks to the Alder Street Scouts, who showed up uninvited with rakes and left the whole east side tidier than we found it.

We also welcomed six new gardeners from the waiting list. If you see someone unfamiliar looking puzzled at plot 31, please introduce yourself and offer advice. Every one of us was once confused about what a "cover crop" was.

## Putting your plot to bed

Fall cleanup is the part of the season that most affects next spring, so here is a short checklist:

1. **Remove spent plants.** Pull tomato vines, bean stalks, and squash. Anything diseased, such as blighted tomatoes or mildewed squash, goes in the green bin by the shed, not the compost.
2. **Leave healthy roots.** Cut peas and beans at the soil line rather than yanking them out. The roots release nitrogen as they break down.
3. **Add organic matter.** Spread two to three inches of compost or shredded leaves over empty beds. We have a leaf pile behind the shed, and you are welcome to take as much as you can carry.
4. **Plant garlic.** The best window is October 20 through November 10. Order from the group buy (see below), break the heads into cloves, and plant them pointed end up, about two inches deep and six inches apart.
5. **Consider a cover crop.** Winter rye or crimson clover can be sown by mid-October and turned under in April. Seed is available in the shed in labeled jars for a small donation.
6. **Clean and store tools.** Wipe your trowels, oil your pruners, and take hoses home or drain them. Frozen hoses crack.

Plots that remain uncleaned by November 15 may be cleared by the work crew, so please do not leave us wondering.

## Garlic group buy

Back by popular demand: we are placing a bulk order with a small farm in the valley for three varieties, Music, Georgian Crystal, and a mild softneck called Inchelium Red. Prices run about $2 per head. Sign the sheet inside the shed door by October 16 and pay Wanda, our treasurer, when you pick up your bulbs.

## Water shutoff

The water will be turned off for the season on November 1. Please plan accordingly, and drain barrels and rain catchments if you want to prevent cracking. Hoses should be coiled and stored in the shed, not left on the ground.

## Compost corner

Our three-bin system is working well, but we have noticed a few problems. First, please chop up large stalks before adding them; whole sunflower stems take years to break down. Second, no meat, dairy, or cooked food, as it attracts rats, which have already been spotted twice near the shed. Third, the finished compost in bin three is ready to use and free for the taking.

## One last thing

Walking the garden last Sunday, I noticed how many people were simply sitting: on the bench by the sunflowers, on upturned buckets, one man cross-legged beside his kale with a thermos. We talk a lot about harvest numbers, but the garden is also a place where the quiet happens. Thank you for keeping it that way.
