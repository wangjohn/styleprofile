Subject: Big changes at Tallow & Rye (and a new loaf you asked for)

Dear friends of the bakery,

I am writing this at 4:40 in the morning with flour on my sleeve, which is how most of my best ideas arrive. I have a lot to tell you, so I will take it in order.

## The new loaf: Seeded Honey Wheat

For two years, nearly every week, someone has asked if we could make a sandwich bread that is not white and not a heavy rye. After a long summer of testing, we have one. It is a tender whole-wheat loaf sweetened with local honey from Pruitt Farm, with a handful of sunflower, flax, and pumpkin seeds baked into the crumb and a crackly crust on top. It slices cleanly, toasts beautifully, and keeps for about five days in a paper bag.

It goes on sale Saturday, October 19, at $7.50 per loaf. Because it is a bigger undertaking than our usual loaves, we will bake just sixty each weekend to start. If it sells out, we will add more. You can reserve one by email or at the counter.

## Pastry changes

Fall is when the pastry case gets interesting, so here is what is rotating in:

- **Maple pecan sticky buns** (weekends only), made with real Vermont syrup and a laminated brioche dough that takes us two full days.
- **Pear and cardamom galettes**, using fruit from the Oyelaran orchard up the road.
- **Pumpkin morning buns**, which returned this week to scenes I can only describe as excited chaos.
- **Savory scones** with sharp cheddar and chive, back by customer demand.

Some things are leaving. The lemon poppy seed muffins are retiring until spring, and we are discontinuing the plain bagels for now. Our oven schedule simply cannot fit them in alongside the new bread, and I would rather do fewer things well than many things half-heartedly. I know that will disappoint some of you, and I am sorry.

## New hours

Starting November 1, we will open an hour later on weekdays, at 7:30 instead of 6:30. This is a hard decision. We have been losing money in that first hour, and my team has been working long days with little rest. Saturdays and Sundays stay as they are, opening at 7:00. We will also close on Mondays for the winter, as we did last year, which lets everyone catch up on sleep, deep cleaning, and paperwork.

## Pre-orders and holiday baking

Thanksgiving is six weeks away, and our pre-order sheet is now open. Here is what we are offering:

1. Parker House rolls, by the dozen ($14)
2. Sourdough stuffing bread, cubed and ready ($9)
3. Apple, pecan, and pumpkin pies, nine inches ($28 each)
4. A rustic bread basket with two loaves and a jar of whipped honey butter ($32)

Orders close Sunday, November 17, and pickup is on Wednesday the 27th between 8 and 5. Please order early. Last year we turned away about forty people, and it still bothers me.

## A word on prices

Butter, flour, and eggs have all gone up over the past year. For the first time since we opened, I am raising prices on a handful of items, effective November 1: croissants will go from $4.00 to $4.25, and our standard sourdough from $8 to $8.50. Everything else stays where it is. I have put off this decision as long as I could, and I want you to know I do not make it lightly. Our costs are real, and so are our staff's wages, and I would rather be honest about it than quietly shrink the loaves.

## Meet the team

I want to introduce Imani, who joined us in August as our second baker. She trained in Montreal, has an uncanny gift for laminated dough, and is the reason the sticky buns finally look as they should. If you spot her at the counter on a Saturday, say hello. We also said goodbye to Felix, who has gone off to study culinary arts. He will be missed, particularly by anyone who ever received a free cookie for being nice to him.

## Classes

Thanks to a great response last spring, we will hold two more bread-baking workshops this winter. The first, "Sourdough from Scratch," is on December 7, and the second, "Holiday Breads and Braids," is on December 14. Each runs three hours, costs $85, and includes a loaf to take home. Space is limited to eight people, and they fill quickly, so sign up soon if you are interested.

## Thank you

Four years ago this month, I opened this place with a secondhand deck oven, a borrowed mixer, and an enormous amount of nerve. I did not expect to still be here, let alone to see the same faces every Saturday morning, children grown a head taller, dogs waiting politely outside. You make this work meaningful. Every loaf is for you.

As always, if you have thoughts about the new bread, the hours, or anything else, write back. I read every message myself, usually while something is in the oven.

Yours in butter and flour,
Cormac Tallow
Owner, Tallow & Rye Bakery
