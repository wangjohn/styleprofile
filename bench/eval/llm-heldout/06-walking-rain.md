# Walking in Rain

You have to give in to it. The moment you accept that you're going to be wet, walking in the rain becomes something else entirely. Not unpleasant. Simple.

It changes how you perceive the world. Sounds are different—duller where the rain lands on soil, sharper on leaves and pavement. Everything smells more intensely. The scent of earth, of plants, of wet stone. Colors shift in the way light passes through water. Greens seem deeper.

The rhythm is different too. You move a little slower because you're paying attention to the ground, to where you step. Your pace follows something other than your usual habit. The rain sets its own tempo and you fall in with it.

There's a particular kind of clarity that comes with being cold and wet and moving through space. Maybe it's just that you're paying attention to immediate sensation rather than letting your mind wander. Maybe it's that the discomfort is so present that you stop pretending to want to be somewhere else. You're here, now, in the rain, and once you stop resisting that fact, it becomes acceptable. Even pleasant.

The rain itself is diverse. Fine mist is different from steady drizzle. That particular kind of rain where each drop is almost ice before it hits the ground. The kind that comes almost horizontally because the wind is strong enough. The kind that's warm and soft and almost tropical, which is rare enough in these latitudes that you notice it as exceptional.

Walking long enough, you become something like weather. Your body adjusts. You're no longer separate from the rain—you're moving through it the way air moves through it. One system among others.

I think people avoid walking in the rain because they've built in an assumption that it should be avoided, that being wet is fundamentally a problem to be solved. But if you approach it differently—not as an inconvenience but as a condition, not as something to endure but as something to experience—it becomes something else. A different kind of walking. The ordinary world transformed by water and the way the light is moving.

You can dry off later. For now, there's the road, the rain, and the simple fact of moving through space.
