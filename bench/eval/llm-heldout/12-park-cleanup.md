# Park Cleanup Day

Last Saturday, twenty-three of us showed up to clean the neighborhood park. This is more people than we expected, which says something good about the neighborhood.

We divided into teams. One team took the playground equipment and cleaned it thoroughly—scrubbing surfaces, clearing out mulch that had migrated and collected debris. Another team tackled the main path, pulling weeds that had grown up through the cracks and replacing a section of border that had deteriorated. The third team worked through the open grass areas, picking up fallen branches, removing trash that had somehow blown in and gotten stuck in the vegetation.

The work was straightforward. No one needed training. People just showed up ready to do physical work in the service of something they cared about.

What struck me was the conversation. People who don't normally talk got to know each other. The person who runs the coffee shop worked next to the architect who just moved to town. The couple with the toddler talked with the retired teacher about the history of the park. It wasn't formal or organized. It was just what happens when people work together toward a shared goal.

By noon, the park looked noticeably better. Not perfect, but genuinely improved. The areas we cleaned were cleaner. Things we'd fixed stayed fixed. And we'd all spent three hours together doing something that mattered, something that everyone could see the result of immediately.

The park belongs to all of us, and taking care of it together is a kind of civic participation that doesn't require meetings or decisions or complicated processes. It just requires showing up and doing the work. The neighborhood is richer for it, both because the park is literally cleaner and because we now know each other a little better.

We're planning the next cleanup for spring.
